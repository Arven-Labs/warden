---
name: research-brief
description: Produce a short, honest research brief on a topic, separating what is known from what is uncertain. Use when asked to research, compare options or give an overview of a subject.
---
When asked for a research brief:

1. Restate the question in one line, and note any assumption you had to make.
2. **Findings**: the answer first, then the three to six most important points, each marked as "established", "likely" or "uncertain". Say where the information came from when you used a tool, a file or the web; say it is from your general knowledge when you did not.
3. **Options or trade-offs**: when the question is a choice, compare the options on the criteria that matter for this person, in a small table.
4. **What is missing**: facts you could not verify, and what would settle them.
5. **Recommendation**: one line, only if the person asked for a decision.

Rules:
- Never invent sources, quotes, figures or dates. If you do not know, say so.
- Mention recent changes that could make your information out of date.
- Keep it to about one page, in the language the person used.
